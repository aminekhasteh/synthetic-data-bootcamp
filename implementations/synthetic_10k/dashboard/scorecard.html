Synthetic 10-K Scorecard

Six synthetic sets (two methods, three models) and a sample of real 10-Ks, asked the same ten fact and six analytical questions per company. Retrieval and RAG answers are scored over the whole filing; the text is scored section by section.

Key findings

  • Both methods state the facts a question needs. Of the ten fact questions per company, Method 1's filings state the answer for 94–100% and Method 2's for 83–100%; both input figures of every analytical question are stated in all six sets. Real 10-Ks state 79% of the facts and 84% of the analytical inputs in a form the checker recognises; the rest are rounded or worded differently from the XBRL value.
  • Method 1 keeps its numbers exactly. When a figure is labelled as one of the ten facts ("revenues of $X", a "Total assets" row), it is correct 98.5–100% of the time, because the statements are built first and the text is checked against them.
  • Method 2 is close behind on numbers. Labelled figures are 95–97% correct. Some Item 8 tables carry "[prior year data]" placeholders, a generation defect.
  • RAG works far better on synthetic filings than on real ones. Asked through retrieval (BM25, top 10) and an LLM, the synthetic sets answer 62–89% of all questions correctly (fact questions 67–94%), against 28% on real filings (fact 37%). When retrieval finds the passages the answer is almost always right, so this score measures how findable the facts are: synthetic filings state each headline fact in a short, clearly labelled passage, while real ones bury it in long multi-year, multi-segment tables. Synthetic sets are easier than real filings, not better.
  • Analytical questions are harder everywhere. A ratio needs both of its inputs in the top 10 passages, so retrieval finds them for only 32–54% of synthetic analytical questions (12% real), and RAG answers 43–80% of them correctly (13% real). Method 2 on Opus answers 80% although both inputs are flagged as retrieved for only 32%: its long filings likely state the ratio, or the inputs in a form the checker misses (10 companies only).
  • Keyword retrieval beats hybrid. BM25 alone answers more questions correctly than BM25 fused with semantic embeddings on every set (by 2–14 points): the questions ask for exact labelled figures.
  • Realism is still the gap. Most sets write 7–56% of a real section's length. Only Method 2 on Opus matches real length (1.2–1.55×) with the closest vocabulary. No set copies real filings.
  • "Figures traced" was retired from the ranking. Any figure near any statement value passed it, so about a quarter of random numbers would; label-aware "numbers correct" replaces it.

Metric definitions

MetricDirectionWhat it measures and how
Question bank
Questionsshown, not rankedEvery company gets the same questions, whichever method wrote its filing: ten fact questions (total revenues, net income, operating income, total assets, total liabilities, stockholders' equity, cash and cash equivalents, operating cash flow, net margin, operating margin) and six analytical ones that combine two figures (return on equity, return on assets, liabilities as a share of assets, cash as a share of assets, operating cash flow as a share of revenue and of net income). The answers come from the company's statements (financials.json, or XBRL for real filings), never from the text, and are written by qa/fact_questions.py, not by a model. A fact a company doesn't have (a bank's operating income) drops that question and the ratios that need it.
Retrieval, whole filing (BM25, no LLM)
Facts answerableshown, not rankedShare of fact questions whose answer is stated somewhere in the filing: a passage states the value, dollars within 1%, percentages within 0.25 points. A question whose answer isn't stated can never be answered, so this caps every retrieval and RAG score.
Analytical answerableshown, not rankedShare of analytical questions the filing makes answerable: both input figures are stated (each in some passage). A passage giving the ratio itself isn't counted: a percentage that close turns up by chance.
Fact / Analytical / All questions recall@10higher is betterBM25 keyword search over every passage of the set (each indexed with its company, fiscal year and Item), the question as the query. A question is hit when the top 10 passages contain its answer: a passage stating the value, or for an analytical question a passage for each input figure. Every question counts, so unanswerable ones are misses and recall can't exceed answerable.
Self-retrieval MRRcloser to realA sentence from a passage, with the company's name removed, is the query; the score is the mean reciprocal rank of finding that same passage among all the set's passages. Low means passages repeat across companies; the real sample sets the target.
RAG answers, whole filing (retrieval + LLM)
Correct %, all / fact / analyticalhigher is betterEach question goes through the RAG pipeline: retrieval returns the top 10 passages, an LLM (gemini-3.1-flash-lite-preview, the same for every set) answers from them only, computing a ratio when the question asks for one, and the number is checked against the statements (dollars within 1%, percentages within 0.25 points, computed ratios within 0.5 points or 2%). The end-to-end score: does a RAG system get the right answer from this corpus?
Retrieval hit %higher is betterShare of questions whose answer is in the 10 passages the LLM saw (the same hit rule as recall@10). When it is, the LLM is almost always right, so correct % tracks this closely.
Abstained %lower is betterShare of questions where the LLM said the passages don't give the answer instead of guessing. Correct + abstained + wrong = 100%, so a high abstain rate means retrieval missed, not that the model made things up.
bm25 / hybridshown, not rankedThe retriever of each RAG run: BM25 keyword search alone, or BM25 fused with a semantic embedding retriever (potion-retrieval-32M) by reciprocal rank fusion.
Numbers
Numbers correct %higher is betterOf the figures the text attaches to one of the ten facts' labels ("net income of $X", a "Total assets" table row), the share equal to that fact's value in the statements (or its prior-year value or change). Sub-lines like "product revenues" or segment rows don't count. A hallucination check on the text itself; not scored under 5 labelled figures. For real filings it is a floor: real text rounds and cites figures the XBRL extract can't verify.
Labelled figuresshown, not rankedHow many labelled figures the numbers-correct check covered.
Realism
Length vs realcloser to 1The section's median word count over the real sample's median for the same section.
Vocabulary overlaphigher is betterJaccard overlap of the section's 1,000 most frequent terms with the real sections' 1,000 most frequent terms.
Figures per 1k wordscloser to realDollar and percent figures per 1,000 words (median per filing).
Diversity and privacy
Repetition across filingsat or below realMedian share of a filing's 8-word sequences that also appear in the same section of another filing in the set. High means the generator reuses the same sentences across companies.
Copied from realat or below realMedian share of a filing's 8-word sequences found in the same section of the real corpus. The real column is a held-out real filing against the others: the legal boilerplate real filings share.
Shown, not ranked
Figures traced %shown, not rankedShare of all figures within tolerance of any value derivable from the statements. Not ranked: about a quarter of random numbers would pass, and it ignores what a figure is labelled as.
Ranking
Suggested set / shadingshown, not rankedPer metric, each set earns points by rank (weight × number of sets it beats); the suggested set per section has the most points over the text metrics, with numbers correct counting twice. Shaded = best synthetic set on that metric.
